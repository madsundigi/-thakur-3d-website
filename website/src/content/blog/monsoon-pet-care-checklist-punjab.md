The single most useful habit is to **dry your pet completely after every walk and every shower of rain** — ears, armpits, groin and between the toes, where moisture hides longest. Do that, check for ticks nightly, and keep your dog out of standing puddles, and you have handled most of what Ludhiana's rainy season throws at a pet. The full checklist is below.

## The monsoon pet care checklist

Print it, or save it to your phone. Each step takes under a minute a day.

1. **Towel-dry fully after every walk or downpour** — ears, armpits, groin and between the toes first; these stay damp longest.
2. **Keep your pet off puddles and standing water.** Carry small dogs past the flooded stretches on Dugri and BRS Nagar lanes.
3. **Do a 60-second tick check every night** — run your fingers over the ears, under the collar, between the toes, armpits and tail base.
4. **Wipe and dry the paws after each outing** and look between the pads for cuts, grass seeds and softened, soggy skin.
5. **Brush the coat out daily** so it dries faster and mats don't trap moisture against the skin.
6. **Wash and sun-dry bedding weekly.** Damp bedding is a fungus factory in humid weather.
7. **Don't skip the monthly tick-and-flea routine** — the monsoon is peak season, not the time to pause it.
8. **Book a monsoon comfort trim** if your dog has a long belly or feathered legs that stay wet for hours.
9. **Keep vaccinations current** and ask your vet whether a leptospirosis shot suits your pet and area.

## Why each step matters

**Full drying stops fungus.** Punjab's July–September humidity keeps a coat damp for hours, and yeast and ringworm thrive in exactly that. The thick-coated breeds we groom — Labradors, Golden Retrievers, Shih Tzus — are the slowest to dry, so towel them down well and, where you can, blow-dry on a cool setting.

**The puddle warning: leptospirosis.** This is the one most owners miss. Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection spread in the urine of rats and other animals; it collects in standing water and wet soil, and warm, wet months are its peak. Dogs catch it by drinking from or wading through contaminated puddles. It can damage the kidneys and liver, and it can also spread to people. Early signs are vague — fever, vomiting, loss of appetite, lethargy, muscle pain, sometimes dark or reddish urine. If you see those after monsoon exposure, a [home vet visit](/ludhiana/vet-at-home/) (₹699) is the right first call, and tell the vet your dog was near standing water. There is a vaccine; ask your vet whether it fits your pet and locality.

**Nightly tick checks catch the spike early.** Ticks run high through Punjab's pre-monsoon and monsoon months and can carry tick fever. Our [Ludhiana tick prevention calendar](/blog/tick-season-ludhiana-prevention-calendar/) has the month-by-month view. When you find one, pull it straight out with tweezers held close to the skin — never burn it, never dab kerosene or petrol, and never crush it. Those remedies only push more infected fluid into your dog.

**Choose anti-tick products with the vet, not the shop.** Dosing is specific to species and body weight, so a strength that suits a Labrador can harm a Pomeranian. And one hard rule: **never put a dog's permethrin spot-on on a cat** — cats cannot process permethrin, and even licking a freshly treated dog can poison them. We [compare spot-on, collar and anti-tick bath](/blog/best-tick-flea-prevention-india/) in a separate guide; whichever you pick, run it past your vet first.

**Paw care prevents the monsoon limp.** Wet pads soften and split, and grass seeds lodge between them on overgrown verges. A quick wipe-and-check after every walk, plus trimming the hair between the pads, keeps small problems small.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I keep my dog tick-free during the Punjab monsoon?**
Dry the coat fully after every walk, do a 60-second tick check each night over the ears, toes and collar line, and keep up a monthly tick-and-flea treatment — the monsoon is peak tick season, not the time to pause it. If you are finding several ticks a night, a one-off professional treatment clears the load faster than daily picking.

**Is puddle water really dangerous for pets?**
Yes. Standing water can carry leptospirosis, a bacterial infection spread in animal urine that peaks in warm, wet months and can pass to people too. Keep your dog from drinking or wading through puddles, and see a vet if it turns feverish, off its food or lethargic after monsoon exposure.

**Dog ko tick ho gaye hain, kya karein?**
Pull each tick straight out with tweezers held close to the skin, and do not burn it or use kerosene. If you are finding them every night, that is an infestation — a tick treatment at home is the quicker fix, and a vet check rules out tick fever.

**Why does my dog smell musty in the rainy season?**
A musty smell usually means a damp coat has started growing yeast or fungus, often in the ears or skin folds. Dry the coat fully after every outing and keep the bedding dry. If the smell stays, with redness, itching or hair loss, have a vet look — a medicated bath is only safe on a vet's advice.

**Do indoor cats need monsoon care too?**
Yes. Cats pick up fungal skin infections and the odd tick in humid weather, and they are far more sensitive than dogs — never use a dog's permethrin tick product on a cat. Keep them dry, comb the coat out, and ask your vet for a cat-safe option.
