In Punjab the risk tends to climb through the warm, humid May-to-September stretch, when ticks are most active. Diagnosing and treating it is a vet's job — a blood test is the only way to be sure. ## Tick fever symptoms in dogs, at a glance

If you learn one thing, learn these five signs — they are what sends most Ludhiana families to the vet. Any two together, especially after a tick bite, mean it is time to get your dog checked.

| Warning sign | What it looks like at home |
|---|---|
| Lethargy | Sleeping all day, no interest in walks, food or play |
| Pale gums | Gums look white or grey instead of healthy pink |
| Fever | Dull, shivery, warm to the touch; nose may be dry |
| Dark urine | Urine the colour of strong tea or cola |
| Appetite loss | Skipping meals for more than a day |

Other signs can include yellow (jaundiced) gums or eyes, weakness, nosebleeds, and bruising on the belly or gums. A stable dog can be seen at home; a collapsing or very pale dog needs an animal hospital straight away.

## What tick fever actually is

Tick fever is an umbrella name, not one disease — and that matters, because the two common ones in India are treated differently. Both are spread mainly by the brown dog tick (*Rhipicephalus sanguineus*), the hard brown tick you find around the ears, toes and collar.

- **Babesiosis** is caused by a tiny parasite that destroys red blood cells. That is why the tell-tale signs are pale gums, dark urine and jaundice — the dog is becoming anaemic.
- **Ehrlichiosis** is a bacterial infection that drops the platelet count, so it tends to show as fever, nosebleeds, bruising and low energy.

A dog can even carry both at once. Because the symptoms overlap, no vet can tell them apart just by looking — a blood smear or PCR test names the organism, and that decides the medicine.

## How tick fever is treated

Caught early, tick fever usually responds well — which is the best reason not to delay. Treatment depends on which infection the blood test finds:

1. **Ehrlichiosis** is treated with a course of antibiotics (vets commonly use doxycycline), run for the full length the vet sets — not stopped the day the dog perks up.
2. **Babesiosis** needs anti-parasite medicines instead; antibiotics alone will not clear it, which is exactly why guessing is risky.
3. **Supportive care** — fluids, and for a badly anaemic dog sometimes a blood transfusion — is given alongside, and a transfusion means a clinic or hospital, not a home visit.

Early, correctly-chosen treatment often turns a dog around within days. A late or long-standing (chronic) case is much harder, so the honest advice is simple: test early, treat fully, and recheck when the vet asks.

## Red flags: when to see a vet immediately

Some signs mean do not wait for a booking slot — go to the nearest animal hospital the same day:

- **White or very pale gums,** or yellow gums and eyes
- **Collapse, or a dog too weak to stand**
- **Dark red or brown urine,** or blood in urine or stool
- **Repeated vomiting, or a fever with refusal to eat or drink**
- **Nosebleeds or unexplained bruising**

One more warning: never give human fever or pain medicines such as paracetamol or ibuprofen. They are toxic to dogs and can make the bleeding from tick fever worse. We run home visits 9am–7pm, not a 24/7 emergency line, so for a night-time crisis head straight to an animal hospital.

## How to prevent tick fever

You cannot vaccinate against tick fever, so prevention is all about the ticks — stop the bite and you stop the disease. Three habits cover most of it:

1. **Check nightly in season.** Run your hands over ears, toes, under the collar and the groin; if you find one, remove it properly — our guide on [how to remove a tick safely](/blog/remove-tick-from-dog-safely/) shows the right way and the home remedies to avoid.
2. **Use a vet-guided tick preventive.** Spot-ons, collars, oral tablets and anti-tick baths all work, but the dose is specific to your pet's species and weight — a dog's product can seriously poison a cat (many contain permethrin), so never share products between pets, and keep a freshly treated dog away from cats until it dries.
3. **Treat every pet and the bedding,** not just the dog with symptoms, because the same ticks move around the house.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the first symptoms of tick fever in a dog?**
Usually lethargy and a drop in appetite, followed by a high fever and pale gums, often a week or two after a tick bite. Dark, tea-coloured urine is a strong warning sign. None of these confirm tick fever on their own, so a vet and a blood test are the next step.

**Is tick fever in dogs curable?**
Yes, especially when it is caught early. Once a blood test names the infection, the vet gives the matching medicine — antibiotics for ehrlichiosis, anti-parasite drugs for babesiosis — plus supportive care. Late or long-standing cases are harder to clear, which is why early treatment matters so much.

**How long does recovery take?**
Many dogs start to improve within a few days of the right treatment, but a badly anaemic dog takes longer and may need extra support. Always finish the full course the vet prescribes, even after your dog looks normal again, and go back for the recheck.

**Mere dog ko bukhar aur sust lag raha hai — tick fever ho sakta hai?**
It is possible, especially after a tick bite in the warm months. Do not wait and watch — the only way to know is a blood test at the vet, and tick fever gets harder to treat the longer it runs.

**Can tick fever spread to my other pets or to me?**
Not from petting or sharing a home — it spreads through tick bites, not casual contact. But the same ticks that infected one dog can bite your other pets, and some tick-borne germs can affect people through bites too, so clear the ticks, treat every pet, and see your vet.
