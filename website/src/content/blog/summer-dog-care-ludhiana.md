Good dog summer care in India comes down to one rule: keep your dog out of the midday heat. In Ludhiana, April to June runs a dry 38–45°C, and a dog cannot sweat the way people do — it cools itself mainly by panting, which stops coping in that kind of heat. So walk only in the cool hours (before 8am or after 7pm), test the road with your hand before you set out, keep water and shade constant, and learn the signs of heatstroke, because it can turn fatal within minutes. A healthy dog's body temperature is 38–39°C; heatstroke sets in around 41°C.

This guide is general information. Heatstroke is a medical emergency, and the steps below are first aid to buy time, not a substitute for a vet — so call one the moment you suspect it, and confirm your dog's summer routine with your own vet.

## Summer in Ludhiana: what changes for your dog

The problem is not just the number on the thermometer, it is how a dog loses heat. Panting works well up to a point; in a dry 42°C it barely keeps up. By late May the dry *loo* wind pushes afternoon highs past 44°C, and the tarmac on roads in areas like Sarabha Nagar and Dugri stays hot long after sunset — which is why an "evening" walk at 6pm can still burn paw pads.

Some dogs are in far more danger than others. Flat-faced breeds such as Pugs cannot pant efficiently, so they overheat first. Puppies, senior dogs and overweight dogs follow close behind. Thick double-coated breeds — the Labradors, Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds that are so common in Punjab homes — hold heat in their undercoat and struggle on a long walk.

## Your dog's summer daily routine

A simple checklist keeps the whole household on the same page:

1. **Do the 5-second tarmac test.** Press the back of your hand flat on the road for five seconds. If you have to lift it, your dog's paws will burn on it — wait, or walk on grass.
2. **Walk in the cool hours only.** Before 8am or after 7pm in April, earlier and later through May and June.
3. **Carry water on every walk** and offer it in the shade; keep a full, clean bowl at home all day.
4. **Give shade and airflow indoors** — a fan, a cooler, or an air-conditioned room through the worst afternoon hours.
5. **Never leave a dog in a parked car**, not even for two minutes with the window cracked. The inside heats far faster than the air outside.
6. **Groom, don't shave.** A double coat insulates against heat as much as cold, and shaving invites sunburn and worse overheating. Book a [summer deshedding groom](/ludhiana/dog-grooming/) instead — a Premium Spa Groom from ₹1,799 removes the dead undercoat without stripping the protection.

## Heatstroke: the warning signs and first aid

Learn the signs, because a dog hides illness until it is suddenly very unwell.

**Warning signs — act at the first one:** heavy, frantic panting that will not settle; thick, ropey drool; gums that turn bright red (and later pale or bluish); a hot, dry nose and skin; vomiting or diarrhoea; stumbling, confusion or weakness; and in severe cases collapse, seizures or unconsciousness. A healthy dog runs 38–39°C; heatstroke begins around 41°C.

**First aid, in order:**

1. **Move the dog to shade or an air-conditioned room** immediately, out of the sun and off hot ground.
2. **Pour cool or room-temperature water** over the belly, armpits, groin and inner thighs — the areas with thin fur. Avoid pouring it over the head.
3. **Add airflow** with a fan or an open car window and AC on the way.
4. **Offer small sips of cool water** only if the dog is alert and swallowing; never force water into its mouth.
5. **Stop active cooling once the heavy panting eases** (roughly 40°C if you can measure), so you don't over-cool.
6. **Go to a vet right away**, even if the dog looks recovered — heatstroke can cause internal damage you cannot see.

**Do not** use ice or ice-cold water: it clamps the skin's blood vessels shut and traps heat in the core. **Do not** wrap the dog tightly in a soaked blanket (it seals heat in), and **do not** rub spirit or alcohol on the paw pads. A same-week [home vet visit](/ludhiana/vet-at-home/) is worth it for any dog that has had a heat scare.

## Frequently asked questions

**What temperature is too hot to walk a dog in Ludhiana?**
The risk climbs fast once the air is above about 32–34°C, and the May–June 40°C-plus afternoons are dangerous for any dog. Go by the hour, not just the thermometer: before 8am or after 7pm in April, earlier and later as the heat peaks. Always test the road with your hand first.

**What are the first signs of heatstroke in a dog?**
Heavy, frantic panting that won't settle, thick ropey drool, and gums turning bright red (later pale or bluish), then wobbliness, vomiting, collapse or seizures. A healthy dog sits around 38–39°C and heatstroke starts near 41°C, so treat any of these as an emergency and start cooling at once.

**Should I put my dog in ice water for heatstroke?**
No. Ice or ice-cold water makes the skin's blood vessels clamp shut and traps heat in the core. Use cool or room-temperature water over the belly, armpits and inner thighs, add a fan, and get to a vet. Never wrap the dog tightly in a wet blanket either, as that seals heat in.

**Should I shave my dog for the summer?**
Usually not. A double coat on a Labrador, Golden Retriever or German Shepherd insulates against heat as well as cold, and shaving can cause sunburn and worse overheating. A professional deshedding groom removes the dead undercoat without losing that protection.

**Which dogs are most at risk in Punjab's heat?**
Flat-faced breeds like Pugs, plus puppies, senior dogs, overweight dogs and thick-coated breeds. They overheat faster and show trouble sooner, so they need the strictest cool-hours-only rule and the shortest walks.
