The best tick and flea prevention in India for most healthy dogs is a vet-chosen monthly product — an oral chew given by mouth or a spot-on applied to the skin — with a long-acting collar as a strong alternative and an anti-tick bath only as a knockdown, never the whole plan. There is no single winner for every dog. The right pick turns on weight, age, whether a cat shares the home, and how much tick pressure your area sees — and in Ludhiana that pressure builds through the dry April–June heat and stays high into the monsoon. Below, each option compared plainly.

**The short version**

- **Oral chews** kill ticks fast, last 1–3 months, and leave no residue — the easiest choice if a cat shares the home.
- **Spot-on drops** go on the skin, usually monthly; keep other pets from licking them until dry.
- **Collars** give the longest single cover (several months) and the least upkeep; fit snug and stop the dog chewing them.
- **An anti-tick bath** clears the ticks on your dog that day but gives little lasting cover — pair it with a preventive.
- **Dose is by species and weight, and permethrin can kill cats** — let a vet pick and size the product.

## 1. Oral chews (tablets)

A flavoured chew given once a month or, with some brands, once every three months. The isoxazoline group (the common prescription chews) kills ticks and fleas quickly once they bite.

- **Pros** — fast kill, which matters because tick-borne germs take a day or two to pass across; nothing on the coat, so you can cuddle the dog and it is the safest option when a cat shares the home; a bath or a swim does not wash it off.
- **Cons** — the dog has to swallow the whole dose; the isoxazoline class carries a rare caution for dogs with a seizure history, so flag that to your vet; the smallest and youngest puppies may be too light or too young for it.
- **Who it suits** — most healthy adult dogs, multi-pet and cat homes, and water-loving dogs.

## 2. Spot-on drops

A liquid you part the fur and drip onto the skin between the shoulder blades, usually once a month. Fipronil-based spot-ons are the ones most widely sold across India.

- **Pros** — a simple monthly habit; easy to buy; some formulas also repel ticks, not just kill them.
- **Cons** — keep other pets from licking the spot and keep the dog out of baths for about two days after applying; a fipronil spot-on kills but does not repel, so you may still see a tick on the coat before it dies.
- **Who it suits** — owners who prefer a topical, and dogs that will not take a tablet.

A caution before you reach for a repellent spot-on: some contain **permethrin**, which is covered in the safety note below.

## 3. Tick collars

A medicated collar that releases its active slowly, giving the longest single stretch of cover — several months from one collar, and some up to about eight.

- **Pros** — the least day-to-day effort and often the lowest cost per month; no monthly date to remember.
- **Cons** — how well it works varies by brand and fit; it must sit snug (two fingers under it) and the dog must not chew it; a collar alone can struggle with a heavy local tick load.
- **Who it suits** — dogs that tolerate a collar, owners who forget monthly doses, and steady low-to-moderate exposure.

## 4. Anti-tick bath (medicated shampoo or dip)

A wash with a medicated, vet-appropriate product that clears the parasites on the dog at that moment. This is the box our home service sits in.

- **Pros** — clears the ticks and fleas on the dog that day and soothes an itchy, flea-dirty coat; a sensible first step for a new rescue or a heavily infested dog.
- **Cons** — little to no lasting protection, so a fresh tick can climb on the next day; it is a knockdown, not a prevention plan by itself.
- **Who it suits** — the first move before you start a longer-acting preventive. ## Safety: dose by weight, and the cat warning

- **Dose is by species and weight.** A Pomeranian and a Labrador are not the same dose, and a cat is never a small dog. Do not split a dog product for a cat or guess a puppy's dose.
- **Permethrin and cats.** Some dog spot-ons and sprays contain permethrin. Cats cannot break it down, and it can be fatal to them — even from grooming or lying against a freshly treated dog. If a cat shares your home, tell the vet: they will avoid permethrin or choose an oral chew that leaves nothing on the coat, and keep a treated dog away from the cat until the spot is fully dry.
- **No product is 100%.** Still run a hands-on check after walks — ears, armpits, groin and between the toes.
- **Red flags that need a vet, not a shampoo:** fever, dullness, not eating, pale gums or blood in the urine. In Punjab's warm months these can point to tick fever, so confirm with your vet quickly rather than treating at home.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the best tick and flea prevention for dogs in India?**
There is no single winner. For most healthy adult dogs a vet-chosen monthly oral chew or spot-on is the reliable base, a long-acting collar suits owners who forget doses, and an anti-tick bath only knocks down what is already there. The best one is the product your vet sizes to your dog's weight and home.

**Spot-on vs collar vs oral chew — which is safest?**
All are safe when a vet picks and doses them correctly. Oral chews leave no residue, so they are often the easiest choice in homes with cats or small children. Permethrin collars and spot-ons must be kept away from cats, and dogs with a seizure history need a vet's go-ahead before an oral chew.

**Is an anti-tick bath enough on its own?**
No. A medicated bath clears the ticks and fleas on your dog that day but gives little lasting protection — a new tick can climb on within a day. Use it as a knockdown, then start a longer-acting preventive so the ticks do not simply return.

**Sabse achha tick aur flea product kaunsa hai?**
The honest answer is the one your vet matches to your dog's weight, age and home. A monthly chew or spot-on works for most dogs, a collar suits low-maintenance homes, and a bath is the knockdown — not a standalone plan.

**Do indoor dogs in Ludhiana still need tick prevention?**
Yes. Ticks ride in on shoes, on other pets and through the lift lobby, and they stay active across Punjab's long warm season, not just peak summer. Year-round cover is the vet-recommended default; at the very least cover the April–September stretch.
