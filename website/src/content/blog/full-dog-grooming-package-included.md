## A Full Groom, step by step

Here is everything the groomer does in a Full Groom, in the order it happens. Every step is part of the one price — nothing on this list is a surprise add-on.

1. **Brush-out and de-tangle** — the coat is combed through so knots do not tighten in the bath.
2. **Bath with dog shampoo** — a proper wash down to the skin, not a quick rinse.
3. **Blow-dry** — dried on a low, warm setting so the coat sets before the cut.
4. **Haircut and styling** — a breed-appropriate trim; a Shih Tzu puppy-cut or a tidy Indie trim, your call.
5. **Nail trim** — clipped and filed back so they stop clicking on your floor.
6. **Ear clean** — wax and dirt wiped out, which also heads off ear infections.
7. **Paw trim** — the fur between the pads tidied so your dog grips the floor properly.
8. **Sanitary trim** — the hygiene areas neatened for a cleaner dog around the house.

That is a full body groom. A Bath & Brush stops after step 6 — the same wash and tidy, no scissors.

## Why each step matters — and what the cost really buys

If you have searched the **full body grooming for dog cost** and found only a vague range, this is why: the number only makes sense once you know which steps are in it. The haircut and styling is the real jump from a Bath & Brush to a Full Groom — it is scissor-and-clipper work that takes time and skill, which is what the extra few hundred rupees pay for. The de-shedding in the Premium Spa is a separate job again: a heavy-coated Labrador or Golden Retriever in Ludhiana's spring shed drops an astonishing amount of undercoat, and a proper de-shed with the right tools is what keeps it off your sofa. The nail and ear work sounds minor, but overgrown nails change how a dog walks, and uncleaned ears are one of the most common reasons for a vet visit. None of it is filler.
