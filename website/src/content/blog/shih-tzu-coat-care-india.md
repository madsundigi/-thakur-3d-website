**Shih Tzu coat care at home** comes down to one daily habit: a few minutes of brushing, every single day. A Shih Tzu's long double coat grows non-stop and tangles faster than almost any other small breed, so a dog brushed daily rarely mats, while one brushed once a week often turns up for its groom in tight pelts. In Ludhiana's April–June heat, when afternoons touch 42°C, a shorter puppy cut keeps this flat-faced, heat-sensitive breed comfortable. Below is the daily-to-monthly routine, the puppy-cut-versus-show-coat choice, honest matting advice, and gentle tear-stain care.

## Why a Shih Tzu mats so fast — and how to brush it properly

Daily brushing is not optional for this breed; it is the whole game. The coat is dense hair over a softer undercoat, and it keeps growing, so loose hair never sheds out cleanly the way a Labrador's does — it stays in the coat and knots. Skip two or three days and those knots tighten into mats at the friction points.

Work in this order:

1. Use a **slicker brush** first to lift the tangles, then a **metal comb** to confirm you have reached the skin, not just the top of the coat.
2. Brush in sections, from the skin outward, paying extra attention **behind the ears, in the armpits, around the collar line, and on the legs and rear** — this is where mats hide.
3. Keep sessions **short and calm**. A dog that enjoys a daily brush sits for it; a dog wrestled once a week learns to dread it.

Don't over-bathe to make up for missed brushing — frequent shampoo dries the skin and makes the coat worse. Our guide on [how often to bathe your dog in India](/blog/how-often-bathe-dog-india/) sets out a sensible schedule.

## Puppy cut vs show coat: what suits India's heat

For most Ludhiana families, a puppy cut wins. The long, floor-length show coat is beautiful but demands daily brushing and wrapping to stay in condition — a genuine job. A **puppy cut** (a short, even trim all over, often with the face rounded) is far lower-maintenance and noticeably cooler in summer.

This is not the same as shaving a double-coated breed. A Labrador or Golden Retriever should never be shaved, because their coat insulates against heat and sun (see [Labrador shedding solutions](/blog/labrador-shedding-solutions-india/)). A Shih Tzu's coat is hair that keeps growing like a Poodle's, so a short trim grows back normally and is a safe way to keep the dog cool.

Even with a puppy cut, respect the heat. Shih Tzus are flat-faced, which makes them overheat quickly. Through Ludhiana's peak summer — April to June, afternoons near 40–42°C — keep the dog indoors under a fan or AC during the hottest hours, roughly noon to 5pm, and walk only in the early morning or after sundown.

## Matting: tight mats are clipped, not pulled

If a mat has gone tight to the skin, an honest groomer clips it out — they do not brush or yank it free. Pulling a tight mat drags on the skin, hurts, and can tear it; the mat also traps moisture against the skin, which in Punjab's humidity invites hot spots and fungal trouble. Clipping the pelt away is the kind choice, even if it means a shorter coat for a few weeks.

The lesson is to catch mats early with the daily brush. Once a dog is matted over much of the body, there is no gentle way to comb it out — the whole coat usually has to be taken short and started again. De-matting is also only ever done on a calm dog, never one that is already stressed.

## Tear-stain care, the gentle way

Those reddish-brown streaks below the eyes are porphyrins — pigments in tears that stain pale fur and darken in sunlight. Shih Tzus are prone to them because of their face shape and the hair that sits close to the eyes. You can manage the staining, but you cannot scrub it away overnight.

What helps:

- Keep the hair under and around the eyes **trimmed short** so tears don't wick into it.
- **Wipe the face gently** with a damp cloth once or twice a day, then pat it dry.
- **Keep the area dry** — constant moisture is what lets yeast take hold.

Avoid harsh home remedies: hydrogen peroxide, lemon or kitchen pastes near the eyes can burn them. And if the staining appears suddenly, smells, turns brown, or comes with squinting, redness or a yellow-green discharge, that is not cosmetic — have a vet check for a blocked tear duct, an eyelash problem or an infection. Confirm the cause with your vet before treating it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I brush my Shih Tzu at home?**
Every day is best. A few minutes with a slicker brush and then a comb to the skin keeps tangles from tightening. If daily is truly impossible, every other day is the absolute minimum — but this is the breed where a once-a-week brush is simply not enough.

**Shih Tzu ke baal roz brush karna zaroori hai?**
Yes. This coat grows continuously and knots within days, so daily brushing is what prevents mats. A dog brushed daily almost never needs a full clip-down; a dog brushed weekly often does.

**Should I shave my Shih Tzu in summer?**
You don't shave to the skin, but a short puppy cut is a safe and cooler option — unlike a double-coated Labrador or Golden, a Shih Tzu's hair grows back normally. Even then, keep the dog indoors under a fan or AC through Ludhiana's peak afternoon heat.

**Why do my Shih Tzu's tear stains keep coming back?**
They are caused by porphyrins in the tears plus the breed's face shape, so they return unless you manage them — trim the eye hair, wipe the face daily and keep it dry. If the staining is sudden, smelly or comes with discharge, see a vet to rule out a medical cause.
