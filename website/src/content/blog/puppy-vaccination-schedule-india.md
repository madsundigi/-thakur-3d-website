This guide is general information. The exact shots and timing depend on your puppy's age, health and the vaccine brand your vet uses, so confirm the plan with a veterinarian before the first injection.

## The short answer: the puppy vaccination chart

This is the schedule most Indian vets follow. "DHPPi" is the core combined shot — distemper, hepatitis (adenovirus), parvovirus and parainfluenza. Some brands bundle in leptospirosis and canine coronavirus and sell it as a **7-in-1 or 9-in-1** shot.

| Puppy's age | Vaccine | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **6–8 weeks** | First DHPPi (or 7-in-1) | Core puppy protection as mother's immunity fades |
| **10–12 weeks** | Second DHPPi / 9-in-1 booster | Builds lasting immunity |
| **14–16 weeks** | Third DHPPi booster + first anti-rabies | Final puppy course |
| **12–16 weeks** | Anti-rabies (if not given with the booster above) | Legally expected and life-saving |
| **Every year** | One DHPPi booster + one anti-rabies | Lifelong, same month each year |

Dates shift a little by brand and by your vet's judgement, but the shape — a first shot at 6–8 weeks, boosters three to four weeks apart, rabies by around 16 weeks — holds across almost every plan used in India.

## What each vaccine protects against

- **Parvovirus** — the big one for puppies in India; causes severe, often fatal, bloody diarrhoea. The main reason the early shots matter.
- **Distemper** — attacks the nervous and respiratory systems; frequently fatal.
- **Hepatitis (adenovirus)** and **parainfluenza** — liver and respiratory disease.
- **Leptospirosis** (in 7-in-1/9-in-1) — a monsoon and puddle risk in Punjab, and one that can spread to people.
- **Rabies** — always fatal once symptoms show, and a human public-health risk, which is why it is treated as non-negotiable.

## Before the first shot: keep the puppy in

A puppy is not protected until its course is well under way. Until about **one to two weeks after the final shot** (so roughly 16–18 weeks old), keep it away from public parks, unknown dogs and places unvaccinated strays use. You can still socialise it safely at home with healthy, vaccinated dogs and with gentle handling — that window matters for temperament — just not out on the street yet.

## Deworming runs alongside

Worms and vaccines are a package in the early months. The usual rhythm: **every 2 weeks until 12 weeks of age, then monthly until 6 months, then once every 3 months for life.** A heavy worm load can blunt a vaccine's effect, so vets normally deworm a few days before a shot. The dose is by weight, so it is worth getting it checked rather than guessing.

## Are home vaccines as good as a clinic's?

Yes, provided two things hold: a **registered veterinarian** gives the injection, and the vaccine is kept in an unbroken **cold chain** (2–8 °C) from pharmacy to your door. A vaccine that has been left warm can simply fail, which is why a carried cold box matters more than the walls around the vet. Ask how the vaccine was transported — a good provider will tell you without being prompted.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should a puppy get its first vaccination in India?**
At 6 to 8 weeks of age. Before that, antibodies from the mother's milk both protect the puppy and can cancel out an early shot, which is why vets wait until this window and then repeat the shot.

**Can my puppy go outside before its vaccinations are complete?**
Not to public areas. Wait until roughly one to two weeks after the final puppy shot (about 16–18 weeks). Until then, socialise only at home with healthy, vaccinated dogs.

**What is the 7-in-1 vaccine, and what does it cost?**
It is a single combined shot covering distemper, hepatitis, parvovirus, parainfluenza and usually leptospirosis and coronavirus. It is billed at the vaccine's MRP; brands and prices vary, so check the printed MRP at the time rather than an old figure. At home we add only a flat ₹199 service fee.

**How many vaccinations does a puppy need in total?**
Usually three rounds of the core booster between 6 and 16 weeks, plus the first anti-rabies, then one combined booster and one rabies shot every year for life.

**Is the anti-rabies shot compulsory?**
Treat it as compulsory. Rabies is fatal once symptoms appear and is a human health risk, so it is the one shot no responsible owner skips, and many boarding and travel services require proof of it.
