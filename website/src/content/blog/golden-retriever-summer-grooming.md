If you are asking **should I shave my dog in summer**, the honest answer for a Golden Retriever — or any double-coated breed — is no, almost never. That thick coat is not the problem it looks like; it is a built-in cooling system. In Ludhiana's April-to-June heat, when afternoons touch 42–44°C, the urge to clip a Golden right down to the skin is understandable. But shaving a healthy double coat makes a dog hotter, not cooler, and leaves the skin open to sunburn. The right summer fix is deshedding, not shaving. Here is exactly why, and what to do instead.

<div class="rounded-lg bg-mint p-4">

**The short version**

- Never shave a healthy double-coated dog (Golden Retriever, German Shepherd) for summer — the coat insulates against heat as well as cold.
- The dense undercoat traps air and keeps skin cooler; the top coat of guard hairs blocks sun and repels water.
- Shaving can cause sunburn, overheating and a patchy coat that grows back wrong (post-clipping alopecia).
- The correct summer fix is deshedding — a thorough brush-out or a professional de-shed removes the loose undercoat so air can flow.
- Only shave on a vet's advice — for severe matting, or a senior dog that no longer sheds its undercoat.

</div>

This guide is general information. If your Golden is a senior, is overweight, or has any skin trouble, confirm the right summer routine with your vet before you change anything.

## The short answer: deshed, don't shave

Deshedding does the job you think shaving will do, without the damage. A clean brush-out thins the heat-trapping undercoat and opens the coat to air, while leaving the guard hairs in place to shield the skin.

## Why a double coat keeps a Golden Retriever cool

A Golden Retriever's coat is two layers working together, and both earn their place. Shaving throws the system away.

- **The undercoat** is the soft, dense fur underneath. It traps a layer of air against the skin, and that trapped air insulates in both directions — holding warmth in during a foggy Punjab December, and keeping hot outdoor air off the skin in May.
- **The top coat**, the longer "guard hairs", is the glossy outer layer. It blocks direct sun and UV, sheds water, and keeps dust and biting insects off the skin.

A dog cools itself mainly by panting and through its paw pads, not through its skin like we do. So removing the coat does not open a cooling vent — it removes the shade and the air layer the dog was relying on. Dogs naturally shed, or "blow", their undercoat as the weather warms, which is the coat's own way of lightening for summer. Your job is to help that shed along with brushing, not to override it with clippers.

## What shaving really does to the coat

Clipping a healthy double coat usually backfires on every count. The problems are well documented by groomers and vets:

1. **It can make the dog hotter.** With the guard hairs gone, the sun reaches the skin directly and there is no air layer to insulate against the heat.
2. **Sunburn and skin damage.** Exposed skin — especially on a light-coated Golden — can burn, and repeated sun exposure raises the risk of skin problems over time.
3. **A patchy, uneven regrowth.** The fast-growing undercoat comes back quickly while the slow guard hairs lag, so the dog often looks fuzzy and moth-eaten for months. Vets call this post-clipping alopecia, and in some dogs the coat never returns to the same texture.

The thinking "it will grow back, so one summer shave is harmless" is the belief this guide is written to retire. On a double-coated breed, one shave can change the coat for good.

## German Shepherds: same coat, same rule

German Shepherds are double-coated too, so the never-shave rule applies to them exactly as it does to Goldens. A GSD's heavy undercoat is what makes it look like it is suffering in 44°C — but the fix is the same: get the dead undercoat out with a de-shed and keep the guard coat on. Shaving a Shepherd brings the same sunburn and patchy-regrowth risks, plus that distinctive coat rarely grows back looking right.

The one honest exception for both breeds is a matted coat so tight that brushing it out would hurt the dog. That is a job for a groomer or vet to assess, not a reason to reach for clippers yourself.

## Deshedding: the right summer routine

Think of summer coat care as removing the dead fluff, not the coat. A simple home-plus-professional routine keeps a Golden or Shepherd comfortable through a Ludhiana summer:

1. **Brush often at home.** A few times a week with an undercoat rake or slicker brush pulls out the loose undercoat before it mats. Ten minutes in the cooler morning is enough.
2. **Never let mats set in.** A mat traps heat and damp against the skin, which is far worse in summer than loose fur. If you find a mat you cannot gently tease apart, leave it for a groomer.
3. **Book a professional de-shed when the coat blows.** A groomer uses a high-velocity dryer and the right tools to clear far more undercoat than a home brush can, in one sitting.
4. **Keep baths sensible.** A clean, well-rinsed coat sheds and dries better; the same deshedding logic helps a heavily-shedding [Labrador](/blog/labrador-shedding-solutions-india/) too.

## When shaving is actually okay

There are only a couple of genuine reasons to clip a double coat, and both go through a professional. The first is a coat so severely matted that it cannot be safely brushed out — shaving is then the kinder choice, done by a groomer. The second is a senior dog whose shedding system has slowed with age: if the old undercoat simply will not come out and the dog is overheating, a vet may advise a careful clip. Neither of these is the routine "summer buzz cut" most people have in mind, and both should be decided with your vet rather than on a hot afternoon.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I shave my Golden Retriever in summer?**
No. A Golden has a double coat that insulates against heat as well as cold and shields the skin from the sun. Shaving it usually makes the dog hotter, risks sunburn, and can cause a patchy regrowth. Brush out the loose undercoat or book a professional de-shed instead.

**Won't my dog be too hot if I don't shave it?**
No, provided the coat is brushed out and mat-free. The undercoat traps a layer of air that keeps hot outdoor air off the skin, and the guard hairs give shade. A dog cools by panting, not through its skin, so the coat is helping, not trapping heat — as long as it is not matted.

**My dog's coat is badly matted — is shaving okay then?**
Possibly, but let a groomer or vet decide. A tight mat traps heat and moisture against the skin and can be painful to brush out, so a careful clip may be the kinder option in that one case. It is a last resort, not a yearly summer habit.

**Do German Shepherds need shaving in summer?**
No. German Shepherds are also double-coated, so the same rule holds — deshed, do not shave. Clearing the dead undercoat keeps a GSD comfortable while the guard coat still protects the skin.

**Garmi mein kutte ke baal kaat dene chahiye?**
For a double-coated breed, no. Cutting the coat right down removes the dog's natural shade and air layer and can lead to sunburn and uneven regrowth. The summer fix is deshedding — getting the loose undercoat out — not shaving.
