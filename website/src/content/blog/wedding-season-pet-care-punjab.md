## Wedding season in Punjab: what changes for your pet

From December to February a Ludhiana home turns into a venue — a houseful of guests, doors opening and closing, loud music late into the night, and food on every table. Add the December cold, when nights drop to **4–7°C**, and a pet already has a lot to cope with. Four things change at once: the noise, the strangers, the escape risk from open gates, and the rich food within paw's reach. The cold brings its own grooming jobs too — our [winter dog care guide for North India](/blog/winter-dog-care-north-india/) covers the fog, cold floors and dry skin that go with the season.

## Your wedding-week countdown

Work backwards from the function and nothing gets left to the last minute.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1 week before | Book the groom slot; a vet check if one is due |
| 2–3 days before | Full Groom or Bath & Brush at home |
| Day before | Nail trim; set up a quiet safe room |
| Function day | Long morning walk, feed early, settle in the safe room |
| Next morning | Tick-check and a calm, short walk |

## Book the pre-function groom 2 to 3 days before

The morning of the wedding is the worst time to groom a dog. A fresh groom leaves the coat a little damp and the dog a little keyed-up; give it **2 to 3 days** and the coat settles, the natural oils come back, and the wet-dog smell is long gone. Which package depends on the coat:

- **Bath & Brush (₹599–₹999)** if your dog is already tidy and you just want it clean and fresh for photos.
- **Full Groom (₹1,199–₹1,899)** for a bath plus a breed-appropriate haircut and styling — the show-ready look most families want for the function.
- **Premium Spa Groom (₹1,799–₹2,799)** for heavy shedders, so your Labrador or Golden Retriever is not leaving fur on every guest's lap.

## A dhol-and-DJ noise plan

A Punjabi wedding is close to Diwali-level noise for a pet — dhol at the door, a DJ till late, and crackers or fireworks at some sangeets and receptions. Plan for it the way you would for a loud festival night.

1. **Tire them out first.** A long walk on the morning of the function burns nervous energy — a tired dog is a calm dog. If you are travelling or busy with prep, a fixed walker for [dog walking in Ludhiana](/ludhiana/dog-walking/) can keep the routine going (a Trial Week is ₹699).
2. **Build a safe room.** A quiet inner room, curtains drawn, familiar bedding, water and a favourite chew. Soft music or a TV helps mask the bass.
3. **Don't force introductions.** Let your pet choose the room over the crowd; guests, however loving, are strangers in its space today.
4. **Tag and secure.** Collar with an ID tag on, and brief one family member to watch the gate — more pets bolt through an open door during a function than people expect.

If your pet's anxiety is severe — constant shaking, drooling or trying to escape — ask your vet about calming options a few days ahead, and never give a human sedative on your own.

## The guests'-food danger list

The kindest thing you can tell your guests is "please don't feed the dog". Keep your pet in its safe room through the main meal, and watch for these wedding-table hazards:

- **Chocolate and cake** — toxic to dogs even in small amounts.
- **Mithai with raisins or kishmish** — grapes and raisins can trigger kidney failure.
- **Cooked bones (chicken, mutton)** — they splinter and can tear the gut.
- **Onion-and-garlic masala leftovers** — damage red blood cells over a dose.
- **Fried, oily food** — a plate of pakoras or rich paneer can set off painful pancreatitis.

## Warning signs — when to call a vet

Most wedding weeks pass without trouble, but call your vet the same day if you see vomiting or diarrhoea after eating something off a plate, non-stop panting or trembling that will not settle, a refusal to eat or drink for a full day, a hard bloated belly, or any collapse. ## Frequently asked questions

**When should I get my dog groomed before a wedding?**
Two to three days before the function. That gives the coat time to settle and the oils to return, so your dog looks and smells its best on the day instead of damp and over-excited straight off a fresh groom.

**How do I keep my dog calm during the dhol and DJ?**
Set up a quiet safe room with drawn curtains, familiar bedding and soft music to mask the bass, and tire your dog out with a long walk earlier in the day. Don't force it to meet guests, and keep its collar and ID tag on in case a gate is left open.

**What wedding foods are dangerous for dogs?**
Chocolate and cake, mithai containing raisins, cooked chicken or mutton bones, onion-and-garlic masala leftovers, and heavy fried food. Keep your pet in its safe room during the meal and ask guests not to share from their plates.

**Should I board my dog during the wedding?**
We don't offer boarding in Ludhiana, and for most pets a calm safe room at home beats a strange kennel anyway. A fixed daytime walker to keep the routine steady, plus a well-prepared quiet room, usually works better than sending your pet away.

**Can you groom a nervous dog at home before a function?**
Yes. The same trained team grooms your pet in its own home, which is far less stressful than a busy salon during wedding season. Book a few days ahead, since function-week slots fill quickly.
