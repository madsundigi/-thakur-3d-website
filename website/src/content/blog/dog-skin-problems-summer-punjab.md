Heat and a damp coat let bacteria and yeast multiply, while fleas breed fastest in exactly this weather. Most cases are treatable once you work out which one you are dealing with. ## Hot spots: the fast-spreading raw patch

A hot spot is the one that can go from a coin-sized red mark to a large raw wound in a single afternoon, so it is the problem to act on fastest. The medical name is acute moist dermatitis: the dog scratches or licks an itchy spot, breaks the skin, and bacteria take over the warm, damp area. Summer and the pre-monsoon humidity make it far more likely, and thick-coated breeds common in Ludhiana — Labradors, Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds — are especially prone because their coats trap heat and moisture against the skin, often after a bath or a swim that never fully dried.

What you can do safely while you arrange a vet: gently stop the licking and chewing, trim the fur away from the patch if your dog will allow it, and keep the area dry and cool. What you must not do is cover it in coconut, mustard or any oil — that seals in moisture and feeds the infection. Hot spots usually need a vet to clean the skin, treat the bacterial infection and, crucially, find the underlying itch (fleas, allergy or a matted damp coat) so it does not come straight back. Keeping the coat clean and knot-free with regular grooming is one of the simplest ways to prevent them.

## Fungal and yeast itching: the musty smell

If your dog smells musty or "corn-chip" yeasty and will not stop licking its paws, you are most likely dealing with a yeast problem. Yeast (Malassezia) lives on normal skin in small numbers, but heat, a damp coat and allergies let it overgrow — and the classic signs are a strong musty odour, red and greasy skin, dark waxy gunk in the ears, and paws stained reddish-brown from constant licking. It tends to settle in warm, folded, hard-to-dry places: between the toes, in the armpits, in skin folds and down the ears.

Ringworm is a different fungal problem worth knowing about, because it is not an itch you can shrug off: it shows as circular, scaly, bald patches, and it can spread to people and other pets in the home. For both yeast and ringworm, the honest truth is that the skin alone cannot tell you which it is — a vet confirms it with a quick skin test and treats the right one. Guessing, and reaching for the wrong shampoo or cream, usually drags the problem out for weeks.

## Flea-allergy itching: when one bite is enough

If the itching is concentrated on the lower back, the base of the tail and the backs of the thighs, suspect fleas even if you cannot find any. In a flea-allergic dog the reaction is to the flea's saliva, so a single bite can set off days of frantic scratching, broken hairs and scabs — you do not need to see a crawling infestation. Punjab's warm April-to-September stretch is peak flea breeding season, which is why this flares in summer.

## Home remedies that make dog itching worse

Searching "dog itching home remedy india" turns up plenty of fixes that do real harm. Before you try anything off the internet, cross these off the list:

1. **Kerosene or lamp oil** — toxic, flammable and savage on already-raw skin. Never, for ticks or itching.
2. **Dettol, phenyl or strong antiseptic liquids** — the phenols in them are toxic to dogs and extremely toxic to cats, even diluted.
3. **Human steroid or anti-fungal creams** — wrong drug, wrong strength, and your dog licks most of it straight off.
4. **Slathering on coconut or mustard oil** — it traps moisture and feeds yeast and hot spots rather than calming them.
5. **Shaving down to the skin** — it removes the coat's sun and heat protection and can leave the skin worse, not cooler.
6. **Over-bathing with human shampoo** — it strips the skin's natural oils and often makes the itch cycle worse.

## Medicated baths: helpful, but only on a vet's say-so

A medicated bath can genuinely help yeast and bacterial skin problems — but only the right one, and that is a decision for a vet, not a shelf. The active ingredient (for example chlorhexidine or an anti-fungal), the dilution and how long the lather must stay on all depend on what is actually wrong with the skin; the wrong medicated shampoo can irritate it further. So treat "medicated bath" as a prescription, not a purchase. ## Red flags — call a vet now

Most summer itches are not a midnight emergency, but some signs mean you should not wait and definitely should not experiment at home. Book a vet promptly if you see any of these:

- A raw, oozing patch that is spreading within hours (a hot spot)
- Constant paw-licking or a strong musty or yeasty smell
- Circular, scaly, bald patches (possible ringworm — it can spread to people)
- Open sores, pus, bleeding, or skin that has turned thick and dark
- Itching so relentless the dog cannot rest, or is off food, feverish or low

If the problem keeps coming back month after month, that is the clearest sign something underneath is driving it, and a **₹699 home vet visit** to find the cause is worth far more than another round of soothing creams. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the fastest safe way to stop my dog scratching in summer?**
There is no safe instant cure, and that is the honest answer. Keep your dog cool and its coat clean and dry, check carefully for fleas, and keep it from licking any raw spot. Do not reach for human antihistamines or creams on your own — the dose and the drug both need a vet. If the skin is red, smelly or spreading, that is your cue to get it looked at.

**Mere dog ko garmi mein bahut khujli ho rahi hai — kya karein?**
First check for fleas and keep the coat clean and fully dry, because a damp summer coat is what most itches feed on. Avoid kerosene, Dettol and human creams — they usually make it worse. If you see a raw patch, a musty smell, bald spots or non-stop licking, book a vet rather than waiting; a home consult is a flat ₹699.

**Can I give my dog a medicated or anti-fungal bath at home?**
Only the one a vet prescribes. The right active ingredient, dilution and lather time all depend on the diagnosis, and the wrong medicated shampoo can irritate the skin further. We are happy to give a vet-advised medicated bath at your home, but we follow the vet's instructions, not a product picked off a shelf.

**My dog has an itchy, flaky, circular bald patch — could my family catch it?**
Possibly. Ringworm is a fungus, not a worm, and it can spread to people and other pets. Do not self-treat it; a vet can confirm it with a simple test and start the right course. Meanwhile wash your hands after handling your dog and keep its bedding separate.

**When is dog itching actually an emergency?**
Itching alone is rarely a middle-of-the-night emergency, but a raw patch spreading within hours, non-stop licking, a foul smell, open sores, or a dog that has gone off food or feels feverish all deserve a same-day vet. When in doubt, it is safer to have it checked than to wait it out.
