## The deworming schedule at a glance

This is the schedule most Indian vets follow. Worms are almost a given in the early weeks — a puppy can be born with them or pick them up through its mother's milk — which is why the first few rounds come so close together.

| Age / stage | How often | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **2–12 weeks** | Every 2 weeks | Heavy early worm load |
| **3–6 months** | Once a month | Gut is still maturing |
| **Adult (6 months+)** | Every 3 months | Lifelong, year-round control |

Pregnant and nursing dogs, and dogs that scavenge or hunt, often need extra rounds — ask your vet. ## The step-by-step deworming checklist

Work down this list each time a round is due:

1. **Weigh the dog first.** The dose is by weight, so a guess either under-doses or over-doses.
2. **Use the right product.** Puppies and adults need different dewormers; a broad-spectrum one covers both roundworms and tapeworms.
3. **Follow the rhythm.** Every 2 weeks to 12 weeks, monthly to 6 months, then every 3 months.
4. **Deworm before vaccines.** Give the dewormer 3–5 days before a shot — a worm-free gut responds better.
5. **Check the stool for a day or two.** A heavy load can mean a repeat dose is needed.
6. **Log the next date.** Put it on your phone, or let the home-visit reminder calendar track it.

## Why the dose follows weight, not age

Dewormers are dosed per kilogram of body weight, and that is the step people most often get wrong. A 2.5 kg Pomeranian puppy and a 28 kg adult Labrador on the same day need completely different amounts — one tablet for both is how a tiny pup gets overdosed and a big dog gets too little to work. Puppies also grow fast, so last month's dose can already be short this month. The safe habit is to weigh before each round and dose to that number. On a home visit our vet weighs the dog and measures the dewormer to it, which removes the guesswork for toy breeds and heavy breeds alike.

## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I deworm an adult dog in India?**
Once every 3 months — a quarterly round for life. Puppies are dewormed far more often (every 2 weeks to 12 weeks, then monthly to 6 months) because their worm load is heaviest; adults settle into the every-3-months rhythm once that early schedule is done.

**At what age do I start deworming a puppy?**
At about 2 weeks of age, then every 2 weeks until 12 weeks. Puppies can be born with worms or get them through their mother's milk, so the first round comes well before the first vaccine.

**How is the deworming dose decided?**
By body weight, not age. The dewormer is measured per kilogram, so the dog is weighed first and dosed to that number. This is why a toy breed and a large breed of the same age get very different amounts, and why a single fixed tablet for every dog is a mistake.

**Mere dog ko pet ke keede ho gaye hain — kya karein?**
If you can see worms in the stool or your dog has a swollen belly, weight loss or loose motions, do not just buy a random tablet. Get the dog weighed and dewormed to the right dose, and if it seems unwell, have a vet check the cause. A heavy load may need a repeat dose a couple of weeks later.

**Should deworming be done before or after vaccination?**
Before — about 3 to 5 days before each shot. A gut carrying a heavy worm load can blunt the immune response to a vaccine, so vets deworm first and vaccinate a few days later. This is why the two schedules are usually planned together.
