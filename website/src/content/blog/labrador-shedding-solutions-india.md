## The short answer: it's the double coat

You cannot stop a healthy Labrador from shedding, because the coat is built to shed — the goal is to remove the loose undercoat on your terms, not the sofa's. A Lab's coat has two layers, and they behave very differently.

| Coat layer | What it does | Why it sheds |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer guard coat** | Coarse, water-resistant top layer | Sheds slowly, all year |
| **Undercoat** | Dense, soft, insulating layer | Dropped in big seasonal blows |

The undercoat is the one that ends up on your clothes. Brushing and de-shedding both target that layer; shaving, as we will see, damages the coat without reducing the shed.

## Why the shedding peaks in spring

Labs shed a little all year, but twice a year they blow coat — a heavy two-to-three-week shed when the whole undercoat turns over. The spring blow is the big one: the dog is dropping the dense winter undercoat it no longer needs. The trigger is mostly daylight, not temperature, which is why a Lab kept indoors under steady light and AC can shed fairly evenly all year instead of in two clean bursts. In Punjab the spring shed broadly lines up with the March-to-May warm-up, just as homes head into the 40°C-plus summer.

## How to brush a Labrador at home

The single biggest mistake is the wrong tool: a soft slicker or a comb made for single-coated or long-haired breeds skims the surface and never reaches a Lab's undercoat. Use an undercoat rake or a de-shedding tool instead, and keep each session short and gentle.

1. Use an **undercoat rake or de-shedding tool**, not a brush meant for single-coated dogs.
2. Work in the direction the coat grows, in small sections, with light pressure — digging in scratches the skin.
3. Brush the whole dog **2–3 times a week**, and daily through the spring blow.
4. Finish with a damp cloth to lift the last of the loose hair.
5. Brush outdoors or on a balcony when you can — far less hair indoors.

A bath with a proper blow-dry also loosens and lifts a lot of dead undercoat, but do not over-wash to chase shedding; our guide on [how often to bathe a dog in India](/blog/how-often-bathe-dog-india/) explains why weekly baths backfire on skin and coat.

## Never shave your Lab to stop shedding

Do not shave a Labrador to cut the shedding or to cool it down in summer — it does neither, and it can cause lasting harm. The double coat is not a jumper you take off. The undercoat traps a layer of air that insulates against Ludhiana's summer heat as much as the winter cold, and the guard coat shields the skin from sunburn. Shave it off and you strip that protection, and the coat can grow back patchy or coarse. A shaved Lab still sheds — now as short, prickly hairs that are harder to clear. The correct summer answer is a thorough de-shed, never a razor.

## When heavy shedding means a vet, not a groomer

Normal Lab shedding is even, all over, and heaviest in spring and autumn. Treat it as a health matter — and call a vet rather than a groomer — when it looks abnormal: bald or thinning patches, red, flaky or smelly skin, constant scratching or licking, raw hot spots, a dull, brittle coat, or heavy shedding well outside the spring and autumn peaks. These can point to fleas or ticks, a skin allergy, a fungal infection such as ringworm, a poor diet, or a hormonal problem like hypothyroidism — none of which a brush will fix. ## Frequently asked questions

**Why does my Labrador shed so much?**
Because Labs have a double coat and drop their soft undercoat all year, with two heavy coat-blows — the big one in spring, around March to May in Punjab. It is driven mostly by changing daylight, not heat, so it is completely normal. You manage it with brushing and de-shedding; you cannot switch it off.

**How do I stop my Labrador from shedding?**
You cannot stop it, but you can control it. Brush 2–3 times a week with an undercoat rake, add a professional de-shed at the spring peak, feed a balanced diet and keep flea and tick cover current. Never shave the coat to reduce shedding — it backfires.

**Labrador ke baal bahut jhadte hain, kya karein?**
Brush two to three times a week with an undercoat rake, book a de-shed groom during the spring shed, and feed a good diet. If you see bald patches, redness or constant itching, see a vet — that is not ordinary shedding. Shaving is not the answer.

**Is it normal for a Lab to shed heavily in spring?**
Yes. The spring coat-blow — roughly March to May in Punjab — is the dog shedding its dense winter undercoat, and it usually lasts about two to three weeks. Even, all-over shedding is normal; patchy loss, itching or sore skin is not, and should be checked.
