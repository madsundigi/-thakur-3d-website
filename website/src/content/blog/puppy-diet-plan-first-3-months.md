A good puppy diet plan for the first 3 months comes down to one rule: **four small meals a day of puppy-specific food**, starting from the mother's milk until about 8 weeks, then softened puppy kibble as the main food. A pup under 12 weeks cannot handle one or two big adult-style meals — its stomach is tiny and its blood sugar drops fast if it goes too long without eating. How much you give scales with your pup's breed size and current weight, and feeding runs hand in hand with the deworming and vaccination timeline. Here is the full plan, by age and by size.

<div class="rounded-lg bg-mint p-4">

**The short version**

- **Four meals a day** of puppy-specific food from weaning until about 12 weeks, then drop to three.
- **No cow's or buffalo milk, no leftovers, no cooked bones** — soaked puppy kibble or a vet-approved puppy food only.
- **Portions follow the feeding chart on the food pack and your pup's weight**, not a fixed cup.
- **Deworm every 2 weeks to 12 weeks** and keep the vaccination schedule — worms and skipped shots stunt growth.

This is general feeding guidance; every pup's needs shift with its weight, breed and health, so confirm the exact plan with your vet.

## The short answer: meals by age

In the first three months, feed little and often. This is the rhythm most vets in India recommend.

| Puppy's age | Meals a day | Food |
|---|---|---|
| Birth–4 weeks | On demand | Mother's milk only |
| 4–7 weeks | 4 | Soaked puppy-kibble mush |
| 7–12 weeks | 4 | Soft, soaked puppy food |

Most pups come home at about 8 weeks already on four meals a day. For the first week, keep the exact food and timing the breeder or shelter used — a new home is enough change on its own — and only then adjust.

## What to feed — and what to never feed

Use a named puppy food as the main diet, and almost nothing else. A growing pup needs far more protein, calcium and calories per kilo than an adult dog, and a proper puppy food is built for that.

- **Puppy-specific food** (kibble or wet), soaked soft with a little warm water for young pups so it is easy to chew and digest.
- **Large-breed puppy food** for Labs, German Shepherds and Goldens — it controls calories and calcium for slower, safer growth.
- **Fresh water out at all times.**

Plenty of Ludhiana homes start a new pup on *ghar ka khana* — bread or soft chapati soaked in milk — but that is the one thing a young pup's stomach handles worst. Steer clear of cow's and buffalo milk (it usually triggers diarrhoea), leftovers, salt and sugar, chocolate, onion, garlic, and cooked bones, which splinter.

## How much to feed, by breed size

Portion size follows your pup's current weight and the feeding chart on the food pack — not a one-size cup. Breed size sets the ballpark and the growth rate.

| Adult size | Example breeds | Feeding note |
|---|---|---|
| Small (under 10 kg) | Shih Tzu, Pomeranian, Pug | Tiny, frequent meals; don't let it skip |
| Medium (10–25 kg) | Beagle, Cocker Spaniel, Indie | Standard puppy food, four meals |
| Large (over 25 kg) | Labrador, German Shepherd, Golden | Large-breed puppy food; don't overfeed |

Small breeds like the Shih Tzu and Pomeranian can get dangerously low blood sugar if a meal is missed, so the four-meal rhythm matters most for them. Large breeds grow fast and must not be overfed or put on adult food early — too many calories too soon strains growing joints. ## Feeding, deworming and vaccination run together

A feeding plan only works if worms and vaccines are handled alongside it. These three are one package in the first months.

- **Deworm every 2 weeks until 12 weeks of age**, then monthly to 6 months. ## When to call the vet

Most puppy feeding goes smoothly, but a few signs mean a vet, not a food change: refusing food for more than a day, repeated vomiting, diarrhoea lasting more than a day (dehydration is serious in a small pup), a hard bloated tummy, no weight gain week to week, pale gums, or unusual lethargy.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should I feed a puppy in its first month?**
Birth to about 4 weeks, only the mother's milk. If the pup is orphaned, use a proper puppy milk replacer from a vet — never cow's or buffalo milk. Solid food, as a soaked puppy-kibble mush, is introduced from around 4 weeks as weaning begins.

**How many times a day should I feed a 2-month-old puppy?**
Four small meals a day of puppy-specific food, spaced evenly through the day. At about 3 to 4 months you can drop to three meals, and down to two by roughly six months as the pup grows.

**Can I give my puppy cow's milk or home food?**
No. Puppies digest cow's and buffalo milk poorly, so it usually causes diarrhoea, and plain home leftovers don't give a growing pup the protein and calcium it needs. A named puppy food is the safest base for the first few months.

**How much should a Labrador or German Shepherd puppy eat?**
Follow the large-breed puppy food's weight-based chart and don't overfeed. Fast-growing large breeds are prone to joint problems if they gain weight too quickly, so a vet setting an exact portion by weight is worth it for these breeds.

**When should I deworm and vaccinate my puppy?**
Deworm every 2 weeks until 12 weeks, then monthly to 6 months. The first vaccine is due at 6 to 8 weeks with boosters to about 16 weeks. Doing both on time matters as much as the food, because worms and missed shots both set growth back.
