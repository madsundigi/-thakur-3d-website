## 1. What the SERP research shows (use this to shape every post)

### 1.1 Who ranks today for Indian pet-care queries
- **Vetic blog** (blog.vetic.in) dominates health + grooming informational queries: 1,200–2,500-word guides, question-form H2s, city-specific landing pages (`vetic.in/dog-vaccination-delhi`, `/vet-at-home/consultation/noida`). No Ludhiana coverage — open gap.
- **Supertails blog** ranks for schedule/cost queries ("Dog Vaccination Schedule in India 2026", "Cat Vaccination Schedule in India") — table-heavy, updated yearly with the year in the title/slug.
- **Heads Up For Tails blog** ranks for breed + how-to queries ("Shih Tzu grooming guide India", "how to safely remove ticks from dogs", "dog vaccination cost in India") — ~1,000–1,800 words, listicle sections.
- **VOSD** (vosd.in) ranks for disease deep-dives (parvo, tick fever, monsoon care) — authoritative, vet-written tone.
- **Virbac India** ranks for monsoon/tick/bathing advice — short clinical advice pages.
- **Thin aggregators** (salonist.io, helpersnearme.com) hold Punjab/Ludhiana local-intent informational SERPs with programmatic, near-empty pages — beatable with any genuine local content.

### 1.2 Winning content formats (copy these patterns)
- **Price/cost guides** rank extremely well (Vetic "How much is pet grooming service... in India", HUFT "dog vaccination cost in India"). Include a pricing TABLE with ₹ ranges, factors, and city note.
- **Schedule/chart posts** (vaccination chart, deworming chart) — a scannable table near the top wins the featured snippet. Add year to title ("2026").
- **Question-form H2s** matching People-Also-Ask phrasing: "How often should I bathe my dog in India?", "What age should a puppy get its first bath?", "Is tick fever fatal?"
- **Seasonal guides** (monsoon care, summer heatstroke, Diwali anxiety, winter coat) republished/refreshed each season.
- **Ideal lengths:** cost guides 1,200–1,800 words; health guides 1,500–2,500 words; seasonal checklists 900–1,400 words; local posts 800–1,200 words. Every post ends with a 4–6 question FAQ block (FAQPage schema) and one CTA to the matching money page.

### 3.1 Dog grooming at home — service page FAQ (12)

**Q1. How often should I get my dog groomed in India?**
Most dogs need a professional grooming session every 4–8 weeks, with a bath every 3–6 weeks depending on coat type and season. Small long-coated breeds like Shih Tzus need grooming more often (every 4–6 weeks), while short-coated breeds like Labradors can go 6–8 weeks. During Punjab's humid monsoon, more frequent brushing and thorough drying matter more than extra baths.

**Q2. Is at-home grooming safe and hygienic?**
Yes — our groomers are background-verified and carry a kit that is sanitised before every pet, so your dog is never exposed to other animals' tools, tubs or waiting areas. In fact, home grooming removes the two biggest stress and infection points of salons: car travel and shared spaces. You can watch the entire session, which salons don't allow.

**Q3. What is included in a basic groom vs a full groom?**
A basic groom (₹500–1,500) covers bath with dog-safe shampoo, blow-dry, brushing, nail clipping, ear cleaning and paw/sanitary trim. A full groom (₹800–3,500 depending on size and coat) adds a complete haircut or breed-standard styling, dematting, and deshedding. You'll see the exact inclusions and fixed price before you book — no on-the-spot upselling.

**Q4. How long does an at-home grooming session take?**
A basic bath-and-brush session takes about 45–60 minutes; a full groom with haircut takes 90–120 minutes depending on size, coat condition and your dog's temperament. Matted coats or first-time nervous dogs can take longer — our groomers never rush a stressed pet.

**Q5. Do you groom aggressive or anxious dogs?**
Yes, in most cases — anxious dogs usually do far better at home because there's no car ride, no cages and no other barking dogs, and the same groomer can be assigned for every visit so trust builds. For genuinely aggressive dogs we ask you to be present and may recommend a muzzle or, in extreme cases, a vet consult first. Tell us about your dog's temperament while booking so we send the right groomer.

**Q6. Can I use human shampoo on my dog between grooms?**
No. Dog skin has a different pH than human skin, and human shampoos (even baby shampoo) strip natural oils, causing dryness, itching and dandruff. Use a dog-specific shampoo, and if your dog has itching, ticks or a skin condition, ask a vet before using any medicated product.

**Q7. Should I shave my Labrador or Golden Retriever in the Ludhiana summer?**
No — never shave a double-coated breed. The undercoat insulates against heat and protects skin from sunburn; shaving actually makes dogs hotter and the coat may regrow patchy. The right summer fix is a professional deshedding treatment that removes dead undercoat and improves airflow.

**Q8. What do you need from me at home for the grooming session?**
Just a bathroom, balcony or washroom with a tap, and a power point for the dryer. The groomer brings everything else — shampoos, towels, dryer, clippers, trimmer and a sanitised kit. The space needed is small; most sessions happen in a regular bathroom.

**Q9. At what age can a puppy get its first grooming?**
A puppy's first bath should wait until at least 8 weeks of age, and ideally 10–12 weeks once initial vaccinations have started, because young puppies can't regulate body temperature well. Before that, spot-clean with a damp cloth. Our puppy grooming sessions are shorter and gentler, designed to make your pup's first experience positive.

**Q10. How do you handle ticks found during grooming?**
If we find a few ticks, the groomer removes them safely with proper tools (never by crushing or with kerosene/oils, which make things worse) and informs you. If there's an infestation, we'll recommend a dedicated tick & flea treatment session and advise a vet check, since Punjab's tick season (May–September) carries real tick-fever risk.

**Q11. Which areas of Ludhiana do you serve?**
We currently serve Sarabha Nagar, BRS Nagar, Model Town, Civil Lines, Dugri, Pakhowal Road, South City, Ferozepur Road, Haibowal Kalan and Kitchlu Nagar, with same-week slots in most localities. If your area isn't listed, send us a WhatsApp message — we're expanding coverage every month.

**Q12. What if I'm not happy with the grooming?**
Tell us within 24 hours and we'll send a groomer back to fix it free of charge. Prices are fixed and shared upfront, and you're welcome to supervise the whole session — that transparency is the point of doorstep grooming.

### 3.2 Cat grooming at home — service page FAQ (9)

**Q1. Do indoor cats really need professional grooming?**
Yes — especially long-haired breeds like Persians, whose coats mat painfully without daily combing, trapping moisture and hiding skin problems. Even short-haired and Indie cats benefit from regular brushing (it reduces hairballs), nail trims and ear checks. Self-grooming alone cannot prevent mats or trim nails.

**Q2. Is it true cats shouldn't be bathed?**
Most healthy short-haired cats rarely need baths, but baths become necessary when a cat is greasy, has sticky dirt, a flea problem, or is an older/overweight cat that can't self-groom properly. Persians typically need a bath every 4–8 weeks as part of coat maintenance. We use lukewarm shallow water, cat-specific shampoo and a calm, quick process.

**Q3. How do you groom a cat that hates being handled?**
At home, cats are dramatically calmer than in salons — there's no carrier, no car, no dog smells. Our groomers use low-stress handling: nails first, towel-wrap technique, breaks when needed, and we ask you to stay nearby. If a cat is severely aggressive we'll stop rather than traumatise it, and suggest a vet-assisted option.

**Q4. What does a cat grooming session include?**
Brushing and dematting, nail clipping, ear cleaning, eye/tear-stain cleaning, a bath and blow-dry if needed or booked, and a hygiene trim. For Persians we also offer a lion cut or comfort trim in summer. Everything happens in your bathroom with a sanitised kit.

**Q5. My Persian's coat has hard mats. Can you remove them without shaving?**
Small mats can be worked out with dematting tools, but large, tight mats close to the skin are painful to comb out and usually must be clipped — pulling them is cruel and can tear skin. After mat removal, we'll set you up with a daily 5-minute combing routine so they don't return.

**Q6. How often should cats' nails be trimmed?**
Every 4–6 weeks for most indoor cats. Overgrown nails can curl into paw pads, get caught in fabric, and make kneading painful. If your cat has a scratching post, rear nails may need less frequent trims than front ones.

**Q7. Do cats in Ludhiana need summer grooming?**
Yes — long-haired cats struggle in 40°C+ heat. Regular deshedding, a comfort trim for Persians, and keeping cats indoors in air-conditioned or cool rooms during May–June afternoons prevents heat stress. Never shave a cat down to the skin; a trim that leaves coat protection is safer.

**Q8. Will grooming reduce my cat's hairballs and shedding around the house?**
Significantly. Regular brushing removes loose hair before your cat swallows it (fewer hairballs) or sheds it on your sofa. During the spring and autumn shedding seasons, a monthly professional deshed plus weekly home brushing keeps both coat and home manageable.

**Q9. Is cat grooming at home safe for kittens?**
Gentle grooming can start around 10–12 weeks — short brushing and handling sessions that build positive associations. Full baths are rarely needed for kittens; we focus on nails, coat and hygiene. Make sure the kitten's vaccination schedule (Tricat from 8 weeks) is underway first.

### 3.3 Dog walking — service page FAQ (9)

**Q1. How much exercise does my dog actually need every day?**
High-energy breeds like Labradors, Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds need 60–90+ minutes of daily activity, ideally split into two walks. Small breeds like Shih Tzus and Pugs do well with 30–40 minutes. Puppies follow the "5 minutes per month of age, twice a day" rule, and seniors need shorter, gentler walks.

**Q2. What does a professional dog walking subscription cost in Ludhiana?**
Our monthly plans run ₹3,000–6,000 depending on walks per day and duration (30 or 45 minutes). That's the professional-walker market rate in India — significantly safer than informal ₹1,000–1,500 arrangements, because you get a background-verified, trained walker, fixed timings and walk reports with GPS tracking.

**Q3. How do I know my dog is safe with your walker?**
Every walker is background-verified, trained in leash handling, heat safety and basic first aid, and walks your dog on a secure double-clip setup — never off-leash on roads. You receive start/end notifications with GPS route and a short report (pee/poop/water/behaviour) after every walk. You'll meet the walker before the first paid walk.

**Q4. What times do you walk dogs in the Ludhiana summer?**
In peak summer (April–June) all walks are scheduled before 8:00 am or after 7:00 pm, because tarmac at midday can burn paws and heatstroke risk is serious — only about half of dogs that develop heatstroke survive. Our walkers carry water on every walk and do the 5-second back-of-hand tarmac test before setting off.

**Q5. Will the same walker come every day?**
Yes — you get one assigned walker so your dog builds trust and routine, with a vetted backup walker introduced in advance for leave days. Consistency matters: dogs walk better, pull less, and relax faster with a familiar person.

**Q6. Do you walk aggressive or reactive dogs?**
We assess every dog in a meet-and-greet first. Mildly reactive dogs are fine — walkers are trained to manage distance from strays and triggers. Strongly aggressive dogs may need a trainer before group-area walks; we'll tell you honestly rather than take a risk with your dog or the public.

**Q7. What happens if it rains or there's heavy fog?**
In monsoon rain we shorten the walk or shift the slot, and the walker towel-dries your dog's paws and belly afterwards — wet coats in humid weather invite fungal infections. In dense winter fog (Dec–Jan) we move walks later into the morning for visibility and road safety.

**Q8. How do you handle street dogs during walks?**
Walkers plan routes that avoid known stray hotspots in each locality, carry deterrent (never harmful) tools, and keep your dog leashed and close when passing strays. This is also why professional walking beats sending dogs out with untrained help — stray encounters are the #1 cause of walk injuries in Indian cities.

**Q9. Can I book a single walk, or only monthly plans?**
Both. Single walks are available (₹150–300 per walk) for busy days, travel or trials, and monthly plans give you fixed slots and better rates. Most pet parents start with a trial walk, then move to a monthly plan once they've met the walker.

### 3.4 Vet-at-home & vaccination — service page FAQ (12)

**Q1. What is the complete puppy vaccination schedule in India?**
The standard Indian schedule: 6 weeks — Puppy DP (distemper + parvo); 8–9 weeks — first DHPPiL (distemper, hepatitis, parainfluenza, parvo, leptospirosis); 12 weeks — DHPPiL booster; 14–16 weeks — anti-rabies, followed by its booster as your vet schedules it. After the puppy series, dogs need annual DHPPiL and rabies boosters for life. Your vet may add kennel cough (Bordetella) or corona vaccines based on risk.

**Q2. What vaccines does my kitten need, and when?**
Kittens get the Tricat vaccine (panleukopenia, herpesvirus, calicivirus) starting at 8–9 weeks, a second dose at 12 weeks, and anti-rabies from 14–16 weeks, followed by annual boosters. Indoor cats need these too — rabies vaccination is effectively mandatory for all pet dogs and cats in India, and feline viruses can enter homes on shoes and clothes.

**Q3. How much does a vet home visit cost in Ludhiana?**
Our vet home visits cost ₹500–1,500 depending on the service — a basic consultation sits at the lower end, while visits that include vaccination, sample collection or treatment cost more. The price is fixed and told to you before booking, and there are no hidden "visit charges" added later.

**Q4. Are vaccines given at home as safe as clinic vaccines?**
Yes, when the cold chain is maintained — our vaccines travel in temperature-controlled carriers from licensed suppliers, and every vial's batch and expiry are shown to you and entered in your pet's vaccination card. The vet also observes your pet for several minutes post-injection, exactly as in a clinic.

**Q5. How often should I deworm my dog?**
Puppies: every 2 weeks from 2 weeks of age until 12 weeks, then monthly until 6 months. Adults: every 3 months for life. Worms are especially common in Indian dogs due to the warm climate and contact with soil and strays, and the correct dewormer dose depends on weight — which the vet confirms during the visit.

**Q6. What are the symptoms of tick fever, and can you treat it at home?**
Watch for lethargy, loss of appetite, fever, pale gums, and dark or reddish urine — tick fever (ehrlichiosis/babesiosis) is common in Punjab from May to September and can be fatal if ignored. A home vet can examine your dog and collect blood for testing; confirmed cases get a treatment plan, though severe cases (anaemia, bleeding) need clinic/hospital care, and we'll refer you immediately if so.

**Q7. What can a home-visit vet treat, and when is a clinic necessary?**
Home vets handle vaccinations, deworming, health check-ups, skin and ear problems, tick/flea issues, minor digestive upsets, post-illness follow-ups and sample collection. Emergencies (trauma, suspected parvo with bloody diarrhoea, poisoning, major surgery, X-rays) need a fully equipped clinic — we'll say so honestly and guide you to the nearest one rather than attempt unsafe treatment at home.

**Q8. My puppy hasn't finished vaccinations. Can it go for walks outside?**
Not on public ground. Parvovirus survives in soil for months and spreads through contact with infected dogs' faeces — unvaccinated puppies are its main victims. Keep your puppy to your home and terrace until 10–14 days after the final puppy vaccination (around 16–18 weeks), and carry it rather than walking it if you must go out.

**Q9. Is the rabies vaccine legally required in India?**
Yes — anti-rabies vaccination is mandatory for pet dogs and cats across India, and proof of current rabies vaccination is required for municipal pet registration, boarding, train/air travel and in any bite incident. The first dose is given at around 14 weeks, with boosters annually (or as your vet's vaccine brand specifies).

**Q10. Why is a home visit better for my pet than going to a clinic?**
Pets are calmer in familiar surroundings, which makes examinations more accurate — no car stress, no waiting room full of sick or barking animals, and no exposure to clinic infections. It's especially better for anxious dogs, cats, senior pets with joint pain and multi-pet homes, and it saves you the Ferozepur Road traffic.

**Q11. Do you provide a vaccination record I can use for travel or boarding?**
Yes — every vaccination is entered in a proper vaccination card/book with vaccine name, batch number, date and the vet's signature and registration number, and we send you digital reminders before the next dose is due. This record is accepted for boarding, travel and municipal registration.

**Q12. My dog seems healthy. Does it still need an annual check-up?**
Yes — dogs hide illness instinctively, and an annual exam (half-yearly for seniors 7+) catches dental disease, heart murmurs, lumps, obesity and tick-borne infections early, when treatment is cheapest. Pairing the yearly booster visit with a full check-up costs little extra and is the single best money-saver in pet healthcare.
